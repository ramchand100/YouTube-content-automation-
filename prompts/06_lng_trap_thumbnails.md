# Thumbnail Concepts & AI Prompts — Episode 06
## "The LNG Trap: How Pakistan Got Hooked on Imported Gas"

Two high-CTR thumbnail concepts. Channel rules: black-based palette, green/white
text, one focal point, 2-4 words that add a new idea rather than repeat the title,
readable at 320px. The title already says "LNG Trap" — the thumbnail should show
the *feeling* of the trap (no gas, unaffordable price, helplessness) rather than
repeat the word.

**Palette:** background `#0A0A0A` · text white `#F5F5F5` · accent green `#1EB53A`
· alert red `#FB5A5A`. **Font:** Coolvetica bold condensed. **Aspect:** 1280×720.
Test at 320px wide.

---

## CONCEPT A — "The Empty Pipe" (the trap made physical)

**Big idea:** a gas pipe with nothing coming out of it. The simplest possible
image of the episode's premise: Pakistan needed gas and there was none. One object,
one emotion, maximum clarity.

**Composition:**
- Centre-right: a large industrial gas pipe end-on, close up, in darkness. The
  opening is dry, empty, a faint wisp of old gas residue inside — clearly nothing
  flowing.
- A price tag or chain is attached to the pipe body, hint of constraint.
- Heavy black negative space top-left and left side for text.
- Dramatic rim lighting on the pipe in deep orange/amber — the pipe is hot
  metal, not delivering.

**Text (on-thumbnail):**
Line 1: `NOBODY` — white, large
Line 2: `SOLD US GAS` — red, slightly smaller
Top-left position, two lines. Blunt. Factual. Sounds impossible, because it was.

**Why it works:** "Nobody sold us gas" sounds like the punchline to a joke that
is not funny. It creates an instant curiosity gap — the viewer wants to know how
that is possible for a country of 240 million people. The pipe image makes it
physical. No text mentions LNG, prices, or Pakistan — the title card does that job.

**Midjourney prompt:**
```
Cinematic close-up of a large industrial gas pipeline end-on viewed from the front,
pipe opening empty and dry with faint residue inside, dark industrial setting, deep
amber and orange rim lighting on the metal pipe body, a rusted chain wrapped around
the pipe, heavy black negative space in the upper left third of frame for text,
dramatic high-contrast photography, photorealistic, moody --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6.1
```
Negative / avoid: `--no text, words, logos, faces, watermark, smoke, flames`

## CONCEPT B — "The Price Tag" (the dollar trap)

**Big idea:** a Pakistani rupee note being fed into a gas meter or valve, with a
dollar sign above it. Captures the exchange-rate trap at the heart of the episode:
LNG is priced in dollars, Pakistan pays in rupees, and every devaluation makes the
bill bigger.

**Composition:**
- Centre: a gas meter or industrial valve with a large USD price tag hanging from
  it, tagged `$70/mmbtu` in red.
- A hand feeding rupee notes into a slot below the meter — the notes disappearing.
- Background near-black, almost nothing else in frame.
- Green tinge on the meter body (stable infrastructure), red on the price tag.

**Text (on-thumbnail):**
Line 1: `THE DOLLAR` — white, large
Line 2: `BILL` — red, same size
Lower-left, two lines. Double meaning: a dollar-denominated bill, and an
electricity/gas bill. The ambiguity is the hook.

**Why it works:** Every Pakistani who has seen their gas or electricity bill double
in two years connects instantly. "The Dollar Bill" is specific enough to be
intriguing and familiar enough to feel personal. The rupee-into-a-dollar-slot
image makes the currency mismatch physical.

**Midjourney prompt:**
```
Cinematic close-up of a worn industrial gas pressure meter with a large USD price
tag hanging from it labelled in red, a hand feeding Pakistani rupee notes into a
slot below the meter, the notes disappearing into darkness, near-black background,
green industrial metal on the meter body, stark dramatic lighting from above,
photorealistic, high contrast, generous dark space in the lower left for text
--ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6.1
```
Negative / avoid: `--no text, words, brand logos, faces, watermark, busy background`

## A/B test plan
- Ship **Concept A** ("Nobody Sold Us Gas / The Empty Pipe") as primary: the
  factual absurdity is the strongest curiosity gap and works without any prior
  knowledge of LNG.
- Hold **Concept B** ("The Dollar Bill") as the 48-hour swap if CTR
  underperforms. It lands harder for viewers already aware of energy price
  increases.
- Keep the title constant across the test to isolate the thumbnail variable.

## Editor notes
- Generate at 1280×720 or larger. Export final under 2 MB.
- Set on-thumbnail text in Coolvetica, not an AI render — always retype the
  words after the AI generates the base image.
- Reserve green strictly for the "gas infrastructure / what could work" cue,
  consistent with storyboard colour language.
