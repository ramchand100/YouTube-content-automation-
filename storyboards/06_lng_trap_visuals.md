# Storyboard & Motion-Graphic Blueprint — Episode 06
## "The LNG Trap: How Pakistan Got Hooked on Imported Gas"

Timestamp-linked to `scripts/06_the_lng_trap.md`. Built for a solo on-camera host
+ editor in **After Effects** (motion graphics) and **Premiere Pro** (assembly).

Three visual layers — every cue is labelled:
- **HOST** = on-camera talking head, medium close-up, dark branded backdrop
- **GRAPHIC** = After Effects pre-comp or animated data visual
- **FOOTAGE** = stock or self-shot B-roll (voiceover continues, host off screen)

Host is on screen for roughly 35% of runtime. Graphics and footage carry the rest.

**Palette:** near-black `#0A0A0A`; white `#F5F5F5`; green `#1EB53A` (stability,
long-term contracts, domestic potential); red `#FB5A5A` (cost gap, circular debt,
spot price spikes). **Type:** Coolvetica/Archivo Black for numbers; Inter for
captions and definition cards.

## SECTION 1 — The Unsolved Reality (0:00 - 2:00)

**[0:00 - 0:10] FOOTAGE — cold open.**
- A CNG station at dawn, "No Gas Today" board across the shutter, a row of
  motorbikes and rickshaws turned away. Ambient sound only: engines, distant
  traffic. No music. No host. Let it sit.
- Cut on the sound of a motorbike starting and leaving.

**[0:10 - 0:50] HOST — hook.**
- First cut to host. Deliver the January 2022 tender story direct to camera:
  Pakistan issued a tender, no supplier responded. Then Russia-Ukraine. Then
  factories went dark.
- Energy: calm, slightly disbelieving at the absurdity of the situation. Lean in.
- No intro. Start mid-thought: "In January 2022, Pakistan put out a tender..."
- ~90 words. Teleprompter active.

**[0:50 - 1:25] GRAPHIC — the price gap.**
- Pre-comp: `openingStats`.
- Two numbers side by side: `PAKISTAN LT CONTRACT ~$10/mmbtu` (green) vs
  `SPOT PEAK 2022 ~$70/mmbtu` (red). Hold. Then a third line underneath:
  `THAT IS A 7× DIFFERENCE`. Source caption: ICIS/JKM Index, 2022.
- Animation: numbers count up simultaneously, then the gap highlights red.

**[1:25 - 1:50] HOST — stakes and tease.**
- Host returns. Deliver the stakes: this is not a one-off crisis, it is the
  end result of fifty years of decisions. "Pakistan discovered gas in 1952.
  Here is how it ended up unable to buy any."
- 40 words. Slow, deliberate. Let the irony land.

**[1:50 - 2:00] GRAPHIC — title card.**
- Cut to near-black. Type-on: **THE LNG TRAP**, red accent on "TRAP".
  Sub-caption: "How Pakistan got hooked on imported gas." Music lifts.

## SECTION 2 — The Paper Trail (2:00 - 5:45)

**[2:00 - 2:20] HOST — introduce the gas balance sheet.**
- Host sets up the math section. "Let us start with the numbers. Pakistan's
  gas balance sheet has one very large problem."
- 30 words. Handoff line into the graphic. Calm and direct.

**[2:20 - 3:00] GRAPHIC — gas supply-demand gap.**
- Pre-comp: `unitEcon` (adapted).
- Two animated bars build side by side:
  `DOMESTIC PRODUCTION ~3.5 bcfd` (green) and `NATIONAL DEMAND ~6 bcfd` (white).
  The gap between them fills in red and is labelled `GAP: ~2.5 bcfd — filled by LNG`.
- `defCard`: "bcfd = billion cubic feet per day, the standard measure of gas flow."
- Source captions: OGRA Annual Report [VERIFY].

**[3:00 - 3:30] GRAPHIC — LNG explainer.**
- Pre-comp: `defCard` sequence.
  - Card 1: "LNG = Liquefied Natural Gas. Cool natural gas to −162°C, it shrinks
    to 1/600th of its volume. Fits in a tanker."
  - Card 2: "RE-GASIFICATION TERMINAL = the facility at the port that heats the
    liquid back up and feeds it into the pipeline grid."
- Simple icons: gas molecule → cooling arrow → small tanker → warming arrow →
  pipeline. Keep it schematic, not 3D.

**[3:30 - 3:50] HOST — introduce contract types.**
- Host returns. "Pakistan buys LNG two ways. One is a long-term deal. The other
  is the spot market. The difference between them is where the trap lives."
- 35 words. Handoff into the contract graphic.

**[3:50 - 4:40] GRAPHIC — long-term vs spot contracts.**
- Pre-comp: `contractComparison`.
- Left column: `LONG-TERM CONTRACT` (green header).
  - Qatar Energy · ~400 mmcfd · ~13% of Brent crude
  - At Brent $80/barrel → ~$10.40/mmbtu · ~Rs. 2,900/mmbtu [VERIFY]
  - 15-20 year duration · price stability
- Right column: `SPOT LNG` (red header).
  - Open market · cargo by cargo · no price certainty
  - 2022 peak: ~$70/mmbtu · ~Rs. 19,600/mmbtu [VERIFY]
  - "No supplier responded" — January 2022
- `defCard`: "mmbtu = million British thermal units; the standard unit for
  measuring the energy content of gas."
- Source captions on all figures.

**[4:40 - 5:10] HOST — the circular debt mechanism.**
- Host explains how the tariff-vs-cost gap generates circular debt.
  "OGRA sets the price Pakistanis pay for gas. That price is often below what
  the gas actually cost to import. The difference does not disappear. It piles
  up as debt — on the books of Sui Northern and Sui Southern."
- 55 words. Conversational. No jargon without the definition already given.

**[5:10 - 5:45] GRAPHIC — circular debt build.**
- Pre-comp: `gapBars` (adapted).
- Animated cost stack: LNG import price (top bar, red) vs OGRA regulated tariff
  (lower bar, white). Gap highlighted: "GAS CIRCULAR DEBT: Rs. 2+ trillion [VERIFY]".
- Below: chain diagram — SNGPL/SSGCL → PLL → terminals → each node with an
  unpaid-bill icon. Show the chain seizing.
- `defCard`: "CIRCULAR DEBT = a chain of unpaid bills between the government,
  utilities, and energy producers that grows because prices are set below costs."
- Source caption: OGRA / Finance Division [VERIFY].

## SECTION 3 — The Field Reality (5:45 - 9:30)

**[5:45 - 6:05] HOST — field transition.**
- Host signals the shift from paper to ground truth.
  "The math explains the debt. But it does not explain why the debt keeps
  growing no matter how many committees are formed to fix it."
- 35 words. Slightly more conversational — leaning into the field angle.

**[6:05 - 6:45] FOOTAGE — industrial estate.**
- B-roll: an industrial estate in Faisalabad or Lahore. Idle machinery, workers
  sitting outside a shut gate, a production floor at standstill. Voiceover over
  footage — host not on screen.
- Voiceover covers: the fifty-year history of suppressed prices, E&P investment
  collapse.

**[6:45 - 7:20] GRAPHIC — declining domestic production.**
- Pre-comp: line chart.
- Pakistan domestic gas production 2012 to present [VERIFY], steady downward slope.
  Annotate: "PEAKED 2012 · DECLINING ~5-8%/year [VERIFY]".
- Source caption: OGRA Annual Report.

**[7:20 - 7:50] HOST — why E&P companies stopped drilling.**
- Host explains the exploration investment logic simply.
  "When you cannot sell your product at a profit, you stop making it. Pakistan's
  gas exploration companies made that rational decision. The result is visible in
  the production chart."
- 40 words. Direct.

**[7:50 - 8:30] GRAPHIC — dual-pricing distortion.**
- Pre-comp: `informalFlow` (adapted to two streams).
- Two streams diverge from a central pipe:
  Left: `DOMESTIC GAS` (green) → Households, Fertilizer, Priority Power.
  Right: `LNG` (red) → Industrial users, lower-priority sectors.
- A decision point in the middle labelled `OGRA ALLOCATION`.
- Caption: "Being allocated cheap domestic gas instead of expensive LNG is worth
  real money. Allocation decisions attract real attention."

**[8:30 - 9:00] HOST — unaccounted-for gas and the 2022 lesson.**
- Host covers UFG and ties back to the 2022 crisis.
  "The system also loses ten to fifteen percent of all gas that enters the
  pipeline through leaks, theft, and old meters [VERIFY]. In a system already
  losing money on every imported unit, losing another tenth for free makes the
  math impossible to fix at the tariff level alone."
- Then the 2022 conclusion: "The lesson from 2022 was not that LNG is
  unreliable. It was that Pakistan had built its industrial economy on a fuel it
  does not control, at a price it never honestly paid."
- 70 words. The key line — deliver it slowly.

**[9:00 - 9:30] GRAPHIC — UFG visualisation.**
- Simple pipeline animation: gas enters (100 units), 10-15% leaks out as grey
  mist before the metered end. Label: `UFG: 10-15% lost [VERIFY]`.
  Source caption: OGRA UFG reports.

## SECTION 4 — The Systemic Domino Effect (9:30 - 12:30)

**[9:30 - 9:50] HOST — zoom out.**
- Host signals the macro section. "Now let us follow the ripple."
- 15 words. Pause. Then cut to graphic.

**[9:50 - 10:40] GRAPHIC — fertilizer domino.**
- Pre-comp: `macroRipple`.
- Domino chain, building left to right:
  GAS PRICE RISES → UREA OUTPUT FALLS → FERTILIZER PRICE RISES → WHEAT INPUT
  COST RISES → FOOD PRICES RISE.
- Each block labelled, falls into the next with a clean animation.
- `defCard`: "UREA = the most common fertilizer for wheat and rice; made from
  natural gas reacted with nitrogen."
- Source caption: NFDC.

**[10:40 - 11:05] HOST — make it personal.**
- Host connects gas price to the kitchen.
  "A farmer planting wheat across five acres cannot absorb a thirty percent rise
  in fertilizer costs without cutting something. Gas price policy and the price
  of bread are connected through a chain most people never see."
- 45 words. Concrete and direct.

**[11:05 - 11:50] GRAPHIC — FX and power domino.**
- Second domino chain:
  GAS-FIRED POWER COSTS RISE → ELECTRICITY TARIFFS RISE → CIRCULAR DEBT IN
  POWER GROWS → LOAD SHEDDING → INDUSTRIAL OUTPUT FALLS → TAX REVENUE FALLS.
- Parallel bar: LNG IMPORT BILL $3-4B/YEAR [VERIFY] shown against SBP reserve
  bar. Caption: "Every billion dollars of gas imports is a billion not available
  for debt, medicine, or machinery."
- Source captions throughout.

**[11:50 - 12:30] HOST — the self-reinforcing trap.**
- Host delivers the trap summary. Slow and deliberate.
  "Tariffs held below cost mean distribution companies cannot invest in their
  pipes. Exploration prices held below breakeven mean no new gas is found. No
  new gas means more LNG. More LNG priced in dollars means every devaluation
  makes the bill bigger. The cycle has no automatic exit."
- 60 words. The intellectual core of the episode. No rush.

## SECTION 5 — The Verdict & Future Outlook (12:30 - End)

**[12:30 - 12:40] GRAPHIC — clean slate.**
- Near-black. One line, white: "CAN PAKISTAN BREAK THE CYCLE?" Hold 8 seconds.
  Music drops slightly.

**[12:40 - 13:15] HOST — verdict.**
- Host delivers the honest verdict. No optimism beyond what the evidence supports.
  "Pakistan cannot exit the LNG market quickly. The dependence is structural.
  Even with the right decisions made today, it takes a decade to meaningfully
  reduce it. The question is not how to escape the market — it is how to manage
  the dependence less expensively than Pakistan has been doing."
- 60 words. Slower pace than any earlier section. Steady eye contact.

**[13:15 - 13:55] GRAPHIC — three forward paths.**
- Pre-comp: `outlook`.
- Three icon rows, each with a realistic timeframe:
  1. `DOMESTIC E&P REVIVAL` — cost-reflective wellhead prices + E&P investment.
     Timeline: `5-10 years if started now`.
  2. `TAPI PIPELINE` — Turkmenistan-Afghanistan-Pakistan-India. Timeline:
     `Uncertain; crosses 800 km of Afghanistan`.
  3. `SOLAR DEMAND REDUCTION` — rooftop solar cuts gas-fired power demand.
     Timeline: `Underway now. Already reducing LNG need.`
- Colour: green for underway, amber for possible, grey for uncertain.

**[13:55 - 14:20] HOST — implications.**
- Direct address by viewer type.
  "If you run a factory in Faisalabad: your energy cost is tied to decisions
  made in Doha and on the JKM index in Singapore. Hedge accordingly.
  If you invest in E&P: the regulatory signal you are waiting for is a
  cost-reflective wellhead price. Watch for it.
  If you are in government: the next spot price spike is not a question of if,
  it is a question of when. The circular debt is the bill for not being ready."
- 75 words. Matter-of-fact. Not lecturing.

**[14:20 - 14:40] HOST — close.**
- "If this helped you understand the gas bill, subscribe. Next episode: [TEASE]."
- End card graphic appears at 14:25. Channel logo, subscribe button. Hold to 14:40.

## Reusable pre-comps (build or adapt)
- `openingStats` — two-stat contrast card (price gap Section 1)
- `defCard` — plain-English definition card (LNG, mmbtu, re-gasification, bcfd,
  circular debt, UFG, urea)
- `unitEcon` — supply-demand gap bar chart (Section 2)
- `contractComparison` — two-column long-term vs spot table (Section 2)
- `gapBars` — tariff vs import cost stack with debt label (Section 2)
- `informalFlow` — dual-pricing two-stream diagram (Section 3)
- `macroRipple` — domino chain animation, reusable across episodes (Section 4)
- `outlook` — three-row forward-path card with timeline labels (Section 5)
- `sourceCaption` — bottom-right citation strip on every on-screen number

## Global production notes
- Every on-screen number carries a `sourceCaption`. All [VERIFY] figures
  confirmed from primary sources (OGRA, SBP, PBS, PLL) before any graphic
  is rendered.
- Host energy: measured and authoritative throughout. The absurdity of a
  gas-producing country unable to buy gas carries its own drama — no
  manufactured tension needed.
- The fertilizer-wheat-food chain in Section 4 is the most important
  viewer-impact beat. Do not rush it.
- Music: low and steady under host dialogue; no dramatic swells. Let the
  numbers do the work. Drop music entirely under the "trap summary" at 11:50.
- Green = domestic gas, long-term contracts, the path forward.
  Red = spot prices, circular debt, the cost gap.
  Keep this colour language consistent so the viewer reads it without thinking.
