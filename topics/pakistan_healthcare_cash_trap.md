# Research Brief: Getting Sick Can Make You Poor (Pakistan's Cash Healthcare Trap)

- **Pillar:** Sector deep-dive (Health). Sits outside the current 4 tool pillars;

## 1. Central Paradox / Hook (Section 1)
Pakistan has almost no health insurance. When people get sick, they pay cash. If
they can't, they sell gold or land, or borrow. So an illness is not just a health
event, it is a financial one that can push a family under the poverty line.

- Hook metric: ~11 million Pakistanis pushed into poverty in a single year by
  medical bills [SOURCE: World Bank analysis, 2024] [VERIFY].
- Supporting: ~13% of households hit "catastrophic health expenditure" (2018-19)
  [SOURCE: SSPH+/IJPH and related studies].

## 2. The Paper Trail (Section 2) — the raw money
- Total health spend ~2.9% of GDP (2022), vs ~5.5% average for lower-middle-income
  countries. Total ~USD 10 billion [SOURCE: Statista/WHO; Commonwealth Fund, 2022].
- Government pays ~38% of it (~USD 3.77B), historically under 1% of GDP
  [SOURCE: Commonwealth Fund; PULSE, 2021-2022].
- Out-of-pocket (money straight from families) ~50-54% of total health spending
  [SOURCE: Commonwealth Fund; OOP studies, ~54%].
- Where the family's cash goes: medicines 50.6% (biggest), doctor fees 12.9%, lab
  tests 8.2%, transport 7.7% [SOURCE: OOP composition study].
- Health cards (Sehat Sahulat): up to Rs400k priority + Rs60k secondary nationally,
  up to Rs1.0M in KP, but **inpatient only** (admitted-to-hospital), at empaneled
  hospitals [SOURCE: Sehat Sahulat Program / PHIMC, 2024-2025].

## 3. The Field Reality (Section 3) — how care is actually paid/delivered
- Medicines are the biggest cost, and the pharmacy is often the first "doctor":
  self-medication, buying part-strips, stopping when money runs out
  [SOURCE: Dawn, "returned boxes, unfinished treatments," 2024; OOP-medicine studies].
- ~83% of out-of-pocket goes to private providers, not public hospitals
  [SOURCE: OOP private/public split].
- Doctor brain drain: ~5,000 doctors left in 2 years; ~40% of new doctors settle
  abroad; ~half of specialists are foreign nationals [SOURCE: Bureau of Emigration;
  JPMA/PIDE, 2024] [VERIFY].
- Medicine prices partly deregulated; two-tier system (private works if you pay,
  public is strained).

## 4. The Systemic Domino (Section 4)
- Poverty headcount rises from ~20.5% to ~27% once medical bills on chronic disease
  are counted, ~13M more people [SOURCE: NCD catastrophic-expenditure study].
- Medical spending crowds out food and essentials, worse for poorer households.
- Sick, indebted households drag productivity; brain drain hollows capacity; low
  public spend is cheap now, expensive later. Fiscal room is tight under IMF constraints.

## 5. The Verdict (Section 5)
- Health cards are real progress but miss the everyday medicine (outpatient) cost
  that bankrupts families, and there is still no real risk-pooling (insurance where
  the healthy many cover the sick few).
- Without more public spending and real risk-pooling, the cash-and-catastrophe model
  persists. Opportunity: micro health insurance, pharmacy chains, telemedicine,
  generic-medicine access, employer cover.
