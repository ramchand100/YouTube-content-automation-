# Episode 17 — Branchless Banking Agent Networks

## Episode metadata

- **Episode number:** 17
- **Topic:** Branchless banking agent networks (Easypaisa/JazzCash cash-in/cash-out agents)
- **Why now:** These agents are the physical infrastructure behind Pakistan's mobile-wallet financial inclusion story, and their commission economics are now facing a direct structural threat from SBP's Raast instant-payment rail.
- **Created:** 2026-08-29

---

## Broad subject

A network of small shopkeepers turned into cash-in/cash-out agents for mobile wallets (Easypaisa, JazzCash) gave tens of millions of Pakistanis their first access to digital money movement without a bank branch — but the commission economics behind that network, both official and unofficial, determine where it actually reached, and are now under pressure from a free, instant central-bank payment rail.

### The Agent Economy That Banked Pakistan's Unbanked

| Field | Content |
|-------|---------|
| Lens | Money and unit economics · Winners and losers · Policy versus ground reality · Informal versus documented economy · Future transition |
| One-sentence framing | A network of small shopkeepers turned into cash-in/cash-out agents for Easypaisa and JazzCash actually gave tens of millions of Pakistanis their first mobile-wallet access without a bank branch — but the same unit economics that made the network spread fast in cities is why it still thins out exactly where financial inclusion was supposed to matter most, and why a chunk of that economy runs on workarounds the companies never see. |
| Central question | How does a branchless-banking agent actually make money on cash-in/cash-out transactions, how do agents' own workarounds — routing cash-outs through personal accounts to avoid the company's cut, and charging for a cash-in service that's supposed to be free — reveal a gap between the official commission structure and what actually happens at the counter, and does all of that explain both where the network reached and why an instant, free payment rail like Raast could now undercut the model that built it? |
| Tentative thesis | Agent commission structures set by the mobile wallet operators made agent density a function of transaction volume and liquidity-float cost, not policy intent — agents clustered where volume was already high and stayed thin in the low-volume rural markets financial-inclusion policy explicitly targeted. On top of that official structure, a real but undocumented layer of agent-side workarounds (routing cash-outs as personal transfers to avoid the company's cut, charging for a cash-in service mandated to be free) means the agent's actual take is often higher than the official model describes. And because Raast increasingly lets transactions skip the cash-conversion step this whole economy is built on, the agent network's core revenue moment is now under threat from the same central bank that once helped standardize it. |

## Recommendation reference

- **Recommended story logic:** Financial, with an institutional throughline
- **Proposed structure:** 7 parts, ~3,500–3,800 words (deeper-topic length — justified by the real added mechanics: official economics, the workaround layer, geographic reach, and the Raast threat, not padding)
  - Part 1 — The Anomaly: the real, large number of active mobile wallets/agents most viewers don't know exists, paired immediately with the rural/gender gap that complicates the "financial inclusion success" framing
  - Part 2 — How an Agent Is Supposed to Make Money: the official commission structure, float/liquidity cost, break-even transaction volume
  - Part 3 — How Agents Actually Keep More Than That: the personal-account cash-out workaround that avoids the company's cut, and charging for a cash-in service mandated to be free
  - Part 4 — Where the Agents Are and Aren't: geographic density explained by Parts 2-3's economics, not by policy intent
  - Part 5 — The Rail That Could Replace the Cash Step: Raast's free, instant transfers and growing merchant/QR use threaten the exact revenue moment Parts 2-3 just explained
  - Part 6 — What This Means for the Inclusion Promise: reconciling marketing claims against the mechanism
  - Part 7 — The Verdict: what the agent model needs to do given the Raast threat, not just the inclusion gap
- **Main counterargument:** this network genuinely did expand financial access dramatically compared to the pre-2010s branch-only banking system — the episode needs to hold that real achievement and the real gaps (geographic, and the workaround economy) in tension, not let the gap framing erase the success.
