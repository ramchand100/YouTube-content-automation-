## Broad subject

Pakistan's power sector carries a large, chronically growing stock of unpaid and deferred payment obligations — "circular debt" — with a structurally linked twin in the gas sector. This episode explains the full machine: the fixed-cost contract architecture it was built on, who fails to pay their share, how the debt spreads between sectors, and why decades of renegotiation and reform keep failing to shrink it.

| One-sentence framing | Pakistan built its power system on a contract structure that pays for capacity whether or not it's used, layered a distribution system that doesn't collect what it's owed on top of it, wired the result into the gas sector's own finances, and has spent three decades renegotiating the edges of that structure without ever touching its foundation. |
| Central question | Why does Pakistan's circular debt keep growing — through capacity-payment contracts, distribution losses, a linked gas sector, and trillions of rupees in claimed reforms — and what would it actually take to stop it? |

## Recommendation reference

- **Recommended story logic:** Structural + Causal (combination) — a fixed-cost architecture (structural) that produces a self-reinforcing non-payment chain (causal) across two sectors, tested against three decades of reform attempts.
- **Proposed five-part structure:**
  - Part 1 — The Machine That Never Stops Growing: open on the scale of the combined debt and the core paradox — Pakistan pays for power capacity whether it uses it or not; close on the central question.
  - Part 2 — The Contract Behind Every Bill: the 1994 Power Policy, IMF/ADB-advised capacity-payment mechanism, take-or-pay explained plainly, and how capacity charges became roughly 63% of the total power purchase price.
  - Part 3 — Who Doesn't Pay Their Share: DISCO losses, K-Electric's NEPRA/Incremental Consumption Package dispute, and lower bill recovery — the distribution-side non-payment layer.
  - Part 4 — The Debt That Jumps Sectors: the power-to-gas cash chain (CPPA-G → IPP → SNGPL), the Liberty Daharki case, SNGPL's own circular-debt burden, and the RLNG-diversion pricing mechanism (drawn from the research already completed for the original gas-power angle).
  - Part 5 — The Reforms That Keep Missing: the 1998/2012/2020/2024 IPP renegotiation rounds and their claimed multi-trillion-rupee savings, tested against what the debt actually did afterward (FY26's Rs364bn gross flow, Rs61bn net increase); an honest verdict on what a structural fix would require.
