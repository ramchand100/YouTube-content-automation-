# Episode 14 — Power and Gas Sector Circular Debt

## Episode metadata

- **Episode number:** 14
- **Topic:** Pakistan's circular debt in the power sector, combined with gas-sector circular debt
- **Why now:** Circular debt keeps growing despite repeated IMF-linked reform rounds and IPP contract renegotiations; the power and gas sectors' debts are frequently reported as if separate, which obscures whether reforms are shrinking the total or just moving it sideways.
- **Created:** 2026-08-25

---

## Broad subject

Pakistan's power sector carries a large, chronically growing stock of unpaid and deferred payment obligations across generators, distribution companies, and the government — "circular debt." A parallel, structurally linked circular debt exists in the gas sector. This episode treats the two as one interlocking system rather than two separate stories.

| One-sentence framing | Pakistan's power-sector circular debt and its gas-sector circular debt are not two separate problems — unpaid bills in one sector directly starve the other of cash, so fixing either alone just moves the debt sideways. |

| Tentative thesis | Power-sector non-payment (DISCO under-collection, unrecovered tariffs, delayed subsidy payments) leaves gas-fired power plants unable to pay their gas suppliers on time; that unpaid gas bill starves SNGPL/SSGC of the cash needed to import LNG and maintain supply, which raises gas-sector costs and arrears, which in turn raises the cost of gas-fired power generation — a closed loop where reforms that treat the two sectors as separate accounts can't actually shrink the combined debt. |

## Recommendation reference

- **Recommended story logic:** Structural + Causal (combination) — a closed-loop, self-reinforcing mechanism between two sectors
- **Proposed four-part structure:**
  - Part 1 — One Bill, Two Sectors: open on a concrete, documented instance where a power-sector shortfall triggered a gas-sector consequence (or vice versa); close on the central question.
  - Part 2 — How the Money Actually Moves: the cash-flow chain — DISCO collection → CPPA-G → IPP payment → gas utility payment → LNG import capacity — and where it breaks.
  - Part 3 — The Loop That Reforms Keep Missing: evidence the two debts move together over time; case examples of "power sector" or "gas sector" reforms that shifted debt sideways rather than shrinking it.
  - Part 4 — One Debt, Not Two: the combined cost, who actually pays it, what's been tried and failed because it addressed only one side, and what a structural fix (treating both sectors as one balance sheet) would require. Tests the counterargument that gas circular debt is primarily a pricing problem, not a power-sector spillover.
