# Research Brief: Why Poor Families Pay for a Free Education (Pakistan's Private School Boom)

- **Pillar:** Sector deep-dive (Education). Sits outside the 4 tool pillars; treat
  as a "Sectors" piece.
- **Status:** researched, ready to script
- **Target length:** 1,800-2,500 words (12-16 min)
- **Language:** 100% English, plain but smart (grade 7-8), define every term.

> Key figures web-verified (Aug 2026). Re-verify anything [VERIFY] before recording.

---

## 1. Central Paradox / Hook (Section 1)
The state offers free schooling, yet millions of poor families pay for low-fee
private schools instead, while 26 million children are out of school entirely.
Why would people with almost nothing pay for something free next door?

- ~26 million children out of school; ~38% of school-age children out [SOURCE:
  Save the Children / UNICEF, 2024-2025] [VERIFY].
- Private schools now ~44% of total enrolment [SOURCE: ADB/RISE, 2017-18].

## 2. The Paper Trail (Section 2) — the money
- Private boom: ~3,000 private schools (1982) to 70,000+ (2015); Punjab low-fee
  private schools 32,000 (1990) to 66,000 (2016) [SOURCE: ADB brief; studies].
- Government education spend ~0.8% of GDP (2024-25), a record low, down from 2% in
  2018; recommended 4-6% (Incheon). Spending fell ~29% in one year [SOURCE: Save the
  Children; Pakistan Economic Survey 2024-25] [VERIFY].
- Low-fee private school unit economics: low monthly fee (illustratively
  ~Rs500-1,500), teachers (often untrained young women) on low salaries
  (~Rs8,000-15,000); model runs on volume + low pay [VERIFY specific figures].
- Shadow education (paid tuition on top of school): 67% urban / 59% rural children
  attend; fees ~Rs1,000-50,000/month [SOURCE: tuition-demand studies].

## 3. The Field Reality (Section 3) — why free fails on the ground
- Ghost schools: ~1,200-1,500 in Punjab, ~1,000 in KP; ~25,000 "ghost teachers" on
  payroll who rarely show up [SOURCE: reporting, 2023-2024].
- Poor conditions (no fans, water, washrooms); teacher absenteeism.
- Low-fee private schools deliver mainly because the owner can fire absent teachers.
- Learning still weak overall: ~half of children can read a grade-2 story; <half can
  do grade-3 arithmetic [SOURCE: ASER Pakistan, 2023] [VERIFY].

## 4. The Systemic Domino (Section 4)
- 26M out of school + weak foundational skills = a human-capital crisis.
- Feeds the low-productivity, low-export economy (ties to Ep on exports/real estate).
- Private boom entrenches inequality: those who can pay get ahead; the poorest get
  ghost schools. Education spend collapsing while population grows.

## 5. The Verdict (Section 5)
- The private boom is a symptom, not a cure: huge demand for education even among the
  poor, but the state has quietly outsourced a basic duty.
- Fix the free system (ghost schools, teacher pay/training, more spend) and regulate
  quality. Opportunity: affordable edtech, teacher training, low-cost school chains,
  assessment tools.
