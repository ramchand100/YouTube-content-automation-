## 1. Approved angle

PSM: The SOE That Ate Itself — how a sequence of individually defensible institutional decisions collectively produced a mill that can neither function nor be dissolved.
Copied from `topics/angles/11_pakistan_steel_mills_angle.md`, approval date 2026-08-22.

---

## 2. Central question

How did Pakistan's only integrated steel mill become a facility that costs billions without producing anything?

---

## 3. Tentative thesis

Each institutional actor — government, courts, unions, lenders — made a locally rational decision. Together those decisions locked PSM in a suspended state more expensive than any available resolution.

---

## 4. Claims required

From the angle's sub-questions:

1. When did PSM stop producing, and what triggered the shutdown?
2. What are the accumulated financial losses and annual carrying costs?
3. How many employees remain and what does payroll cost?

5. What does Pakistan pay annually for imported steel in lieu of domestic production?
6. What were PSM's construction dates and designed capacity?
7. What were PSM's production costs relative to market price before shutdown?

## 5. Raw findings

### Shutdown date and trigger

- **Source:** Multiple Pakistani news sources (Dawn, Express Tribune, SAMAA) consistently cite June 10, 2015.
- **Verbatim (Express Tribune context):** "The Mills shut down on June 10, 2015, and all the plants and machines were shut down abruptly, at once."
- **Trigger:** Sui Southern Gas Company (SSGC) cut gas supply because PSM owed Rs 19 billion in overdue bills.
- **Operating rate at shutdown:** ~33% of designed capacity at time of shutdown.
- **URL:** Dawn, Express Tribune, Herald — multiple articles, all consistent on date.

### Accumulated losses — AGP audit (June 2026)

- **Source:** Auditor-General of Pakistan (AGP) report, released June 2026. Reported by ProPakistani, The News, Bloom Pakistan, Profit by Pakistan Today.
- **Key figures:**
  - Accumulated losses by FY2023-24: **Rs 245.9 billion**
  - FY2008-09 accumulated losses (start of comparison): Rs 16.9 billion
  - Growth: 1,355% over 15 years
  - Average annual net loss FY2009–FY2024: Rs 25.5 billion
  - FY2023-24 net loss: Rs 25.5 billion
  - FY2024-25 net loss: Rs 26.0 billion
  - Government loans to PSM (2013–2025): **Rs 106.2 billion**
- **Loss rate (FY2023-24):** Rs 6 million per hour — cited by search results referencing Ministry of Industries
- **Annual interest burden:** >Rs 20 billion (mentioned in multiple sources)
- **Note:** One search result cited "Rs 600 billion cumulative losses by 2024" — this conflicts with AGP Rs 245.9 billion and is probably inflated by including contingent liabilities or different accounting treatment. AGP figure is authoritative — use Rs 245.9 billion.

### Government payroll support

- **Source:** ProPakistani, January 2025, citing ECC approval.
- **Figure:** Rs 935.78 million approved for FY2024-25 salary disbursements for PSM employees.
- **Interpretation (ESTIMATE):** At ~Rs 936 million/year, the annual payroll cost for the dormant workforce is roughly Rs 1 billion. This is the direct state cost of keeping non-producing employees. Debt servicing is additional and much larger (>Rs 20 billion interest).

### Workforce

- **Peak:** ~30,000 employees (Paradigm Shift; not independently confirmed — treat as REPORTED)
- **At June 2015 shutdown:** ~9,350 employees (Dawn reporting on June 2020 ECC decision)
- **June 2020:** ECC approved retrenchment of all 9,350 remaining employees. One-time severance cost estimate: ~Rs 20 billion.
- **November 2020:** 4,544 employees sacked (Dawn, Tribune, SAMAA — consistent).
- **Total retrenched 2020-2021:** ~5,300 (includes 49.9% of workforce)
- **PSM filed Labour Court case for retrenchment of remaining ~51%.**
- **2023:** "Staff reinstatement: PSM management being pressurised" (Business Recorder) — suggests court orders reinstated some workers.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Current headcount as of 2026 is not confirmed in publicly accessible sources. Court reinstatements, partial reversals, and attrition make the exact number unclear. The narrative in the script that employees "remain on the payroll" is directionally correct but headcount is imprecise. [VERIFY — Ministry of Industries testimony to National Assembly is the primary source]

### Privatisation (2006)

- **Date:** June 23, 2006 — CONFIRMED
- **Court:** Supreme Court of Pakistan, bench headed by Chief Justice Iftikhar Chaudhry
- **Ruling:** Found the privatisation process "vitiated by acts of omissions and commissions on the part of certain State functionaries reflecting violation of mandatory provisions of law and the rules framed thereunder."
- **Action:** Declared letter of acceptance null and void.
- **Significance:** First time a completed privatisation of a government entity was reversed by the Supreme Court.
- **Citation:** PLD 2006 SC 697
- **Sources:** Business Recorder ("Supreme Court declares PSM deal of no legal effect"), ResearchGate academic paper on case, YouTube video title confirms citation.

### PSM establishment and history

- **1968:** Government of Pakistan decided Karachi Steel Project should be in public sector.
- **January 1971:** Pakistan-USSR agreement signed for techno-financial assistance.
- **December 30, 1973:** Foundation stone laid by PM Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.
- **April 1981:** Full operations commenced.
- **Designed capacity:** 1.1 million tonnes per annum.
- **Sources:** Express Tribune ("A look back at history of PSM"), Paradigm Shift, Grokipedia (citing Wikipedia). Consistent across multiple sources.
- **Soviet comparators:** USSR also assisted India's Bhilai and Bokaro steel plants (well-established historical record).

### Pakistan steel imports

- **2024 total iron and steel imports:** US$3.09 billion (COMTRADE database, cited by search results).
- **Finished steel:** During July-May FY2024, imports of finished iron and steel products: $2.062 billion (up 22% from $1.686 billion in full FY2023).
- **Scrap:** July-May FY2024 iron and steel scrap imports: $1.556 billion (up 46.5% from $1.062 billion in full FY2023).
- **Monthly volume:** ~300,000-327,000 tonnes/month for finished steel (Feb 2025 = 326,859 tonnes; May 2024 = 302,412 tonnes) — source: PBS data as cited by Mesteel News and Profit by Pakistan Today.
- **Annualised estimate (ESTIMATE):** At ~300,000 tonnes/month for finished steel, annual volume ≈ 3.5-4 million tonnes finished steel, plus separate scrap volume. Total iron and steel import volume likely 5-7 MT when scrap is included. The script range "4-7 million tonnes" overstates finished steel but is reasonable as an all-products range — revise to "Pakistan imports around 3-4 million tonnes of finished steel annually, plus additional scrap, for a combined import bill exceeding $3 billion."
- **PBS primary data:** Not directly accessed (proxy blocked). All figures are from search summaries citing PBS. [VERIFY with direct PBS trade data access before recording]

### 2014 turnaround attempt

- **Rs 18.5 billion bailout** approved in 2014 for PSM turnaround.
- **Break-even threshold:** 77% capacity utilisation (targeted by January 2015).
- **Achievement:** Mill could not sustain even 60% capacity (set as November 2014 target) due to poorly maintained infrastructure.
- **Source:** Search results referencing PIDE and press reports. PIDE website blocked. Treat as REPORTED.

### Post-2015 events

- **June 2020:** ECC approved retrenchment of all 9,350 employees. Government said one-time cost ~Rs 20 billion. Partial retrenchment followed (4,544 sacked Nov 2020) but litigation blocked full completion.
- **May 2023:** ECC decided government would no longer be responsible for gas obligations after June 30, 2023.
- **July 4, 2023:** Government publicly announced decision to shut down PSM (no available buyer due to heavy losses).
- **Assets at July 2023:** Rs 83 billion.
- **Gas obligations at July 2023:** Rs 33.5 billion.
- **Subsequent:** Search results show "Pakistan steps back from liquidation decision: Steel Mills to be revived" — the 2023 closure announcement was also reversed or paused.
- **PSM Board of Directors** opposed closure in 2024, writing to PM.
- **Interpretation (ANALYSIS):** The 2020 and 2023 resolution attempts both stalled. This confirms the script's thesis: the pattern of deferred decisions continued past 2015.

### Unit economics / cost per tonne

- **UNRESOLVED:** No specific cost-per-tonne vs. market-price figure found in accessible sources. The Rs 18.5 billion bailout and break-even at 77% capacity implies PSM was producing below break-even. The 33% capacity utilisation at shutdown confirms this. But an exact "cost per tonne exceeded market price by X" figure is not documented in accessible sources.
- **Script treatment:** The claim "PSM's cost of producing one tonne of steel exceeded the price it could charge" is ANALYSIS supported by circumstantial evidence (below-break-even operation, progressive output reduction, financial losses). Label it ANALYSIS in the script rather than VERIFIED. Do not use a specific per-tonne cost figure.

## 9. Counterarguments and how they are addressed

**Counterargument 1: PSM failed because of corruption, not institutional design.**
Response: Evidence of specific corruption exists in press reporting, but the structural problems (administered pricing, workforce accumulation, protection from competition) precede any identified corrupt act. The 2006 privatisation ruling addressed procedural irregularities, not corruption. The script attributes outcomes to institutional incentives, not individual actors. Labelling it "corruption" alone would miss the mechanism.

**Counterargument 2: PSM could have been saved with proper investment.**
Response: The 2014 Rs 18.5 billion bailout failed because PSM could not reach the 77% break-even threshold due to equipment deterioration. Private investors would not invest without first resolving the workforce obligation, which cost an estimated Rs 20 billion in severance alone. The commercial case was not viable without simultaneously resolving workforce, equipment, and competition from cheaper scrap-based re-rollers.

**Counterargument 3: The Supreme Court was right to block privatisation on procedural grounds.**
Response: The script does not dispute the legal ruling. It notes that the legal challenge was legitimate while pointing out that the practical consequence — blocking the only realistic exit path at the time — was to defer the problem at increasing cost.

**Counterargument 4: The private re-rolling sector could have co-existed with PSM.**
Response: PSM produces primary steel (flat products, billets from iron ore). Re-rollers use scrap. They occupy different product niches. The argument stands that PSM's primary-steel cost structure was uncompetitive. However, this should be noted in the script more precisely.

## 10. Stakeholder map

| Stakeholder | What they want | Constraint | Decision made |
|-------------|---------------|-----------|---------------|
| Federal government | Avoid political cost of mass redundancy | Cannot absorb Rs 20B+ severance without IMF constraints | Deferred restructuring repeatedly |
| PSM employees / unions | Preserve employment | No severance offer acceptable without legal protection | Resisted retrenchment; used courts |
| Courts | Apply law correctly | Procedural requirements must be met | Blocked privatisation on legitimate grounds |
| Commercial lenders | Recover loans | Government guarantees keep credit active | Extended credit; no incentive to call default |
| Private re-rollers | Operate without PSM competition | Have no interest in PSM revival | Benefited from PSM absence |
| Construction sector | Access cheapest available steel | No domestic alternative; import-dependent | Price-taker in global commodity cycle |
| IMF / fiscal monitors | Reduce SOE drain on budget | Push for privatisation or closure; limited leverage | Pressure but not decisive |
| AGP | Audit and report | No enforcement power | Issued findings (Rs 245.9B report, June 2026) |
