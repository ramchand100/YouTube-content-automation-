## Approved angle

**The Gas-Power Doom Loop** — Pakistan's power-sector circular debt and its
gas-sector circular debt are not two separate problems: unpaid bills in one
sector directly starve the other of cash, so fixing either alone just moves
the debt sideways.

## Central question

Is Pakistan's circular debt actually one problem or two feeding each other —
and why do reforms aimed at one sector keep failing to shrink the total?

## Tentative thesis

Power-sector non-payment (DISCO under-collection, unrecovered tariffs,
delayed subsidy payments) leaves gas-fired power plants unable to pay their
gas suppliers on time; that unpaid gas bill starves SNGPL/SSGC of the cash
needed to import LNG and maintain supply, which raises gas-sector costs and
arrears, which in turn raises the cost of gas-fired power generation — a
closed loop where reforms that treat the two sectors as separate accounts
can't actually shrink the combined debt.

**Thesis refinement from research (read before structure approval):** the
evidence supports a real, worsening, well-documented power-to-gas payment
channel (see Raw findings, "The mechanism" and "The Liberty Daharki case"
below) — but it does not support power-sector non-payment as the *dominant*
driver of total gas-sector circular debt. Independent reporting attributes
the larger share of the Rs3.611 trillion gas-sector stock to domestic
consumer underpricing and RLNG-to-domestic-tariff diversion, a pricing
problem distinct from power-sector arrears (see C011, C013, C025). The
honest version of this thesis is: the two debts are linked and mutually
reinforcing, and reforms that ignore the linkage keep failing — not that
power-sector non-payment *causes* the gas circular debt. The script should
state this precisely rather than overclaim causation. This directly answers
the approved angle's own flagged research risk.

## Claims required (from the angle's sub-questions)

1. What is the current scale of power-sector, gas-sector, and combined circular debt, and how has it moved recently?
2. Exactly how does money (or its absence) move between the power and gas sectors — where does the chain actually break?
3. Is there a specific, documented case showing a power-sector shortfall causing a gas-sector consequence (or vice versa)?
4. Has this loop repeated despite reform announcements, or is it improving?
5. Is gas-sector circular debt actually caused by power-sector non-payment, or mainly by something else (the counterargument)?

## Raw findings

### The headline numbers

- Combined power + gas circular debt reached **Rs5.286 trillion** by June
  2026, up from Rs5.206 trillion at the start of calendar year 2026.
  [REPORTED — The Nation, 2026-08-19, citing an unnamed "official source";
  directly fetched. `[VERIFY]` — the article does not name the specific
  ministry/report this figure originates from.]
- The IMF's third review under the Extended Fund Facility (EFF) and second
  review under the Resilience and Sustainability Facility (RSF) separately
  estimated the power sector's circular debt at Rs1.764 trillion and the gas
  sector's at Rs3.442 trillion as of "early 2026" (Rs1.764tn + Rs3.442tn ≈
  Rs5.206tn, matching the Nation article's "earlier" combined baseline).
  [REPORTED — The Nation, directly fetched, citing IMF review documents]
- Power-sector circular debt alone stood at **Rs1.675 trillion** as of June
  30, 2026 (end of FY2025-26), up from Rs1.614 trillion at the end of
  FY2024-25 — a **net** increase of Rs61 billion. [REPORTED — The News,
  2026-08-19, citing Power Division data; directly fetched]
- The **gross** flow of new power-sector circular debt during FY2025-26 was
  Rs364 billion — 709% higher than the prior year's Rs319 billion gross
  flow. The gap between the Rs364bn gross flow and the Rs61bn net increase
  is explained by government offsets: Rs302 billion in subsidy payments,
  Rs129 billion in loan principal repayment, and a Rs98 billion reduction in
  unclaimed subsidies. [REPORTED — Express Tribune, 2026, directly fetched]
- Gas-sector circular debt stood at **Rs3.611 trillion** as of June 2026, up
  from Rs3.442 trillion at the start of calendar year 2026. Of this,
  Rs1.839 trillion is principal; the remainder is accumulated late-payment
  surcharges. [REPORTED — The Nation, directly fetched; corroborated by
  multiple other outlets in search results]

### FY2025-26 power-sector debt: what actually drove the Rs364bn gross flow

- DISCO operational inefficiencies: Rs262 billion (essentially flat versus
  the prior year's Rs265bn).
- K-Electric non-payment: Rs194 billion added; K-Electric's total
  receivables reached Rs421 billion (Rs197bn principal + Rs224bn accumulated
  markup) by June 2026.
- Lower bill recovery: Rs64 billion (an improvement from Rs132bn the prior
  year).
- Tariff-adjustment delays / WAPDA invoice delays: Rs63-75 billion (two
  overlapping figures appear across sources for what may be the same or an
  adjacent line item — `[VERIFY]` before citing a single number).
- Interest charges: Rs14 billion (down sharply from Rs58bn the prior year).
  [REPORTED — The News + Express Tribune, both directly fetched and
  cross-corroborating on the DISCO/K-Electric/recovery figures]
- K-Electric's Rs194bn non-payment driver traces to its dispute with NEPRA
  over the "Incremental Consumption Package" (ICP): implemented November
  2020-June 2021 under a NEPRA determination dated December 1, 2020, then
  extended July 2021-October 2023. K-Electric says a later NEPRA
  determination "materially departed" from the first and is inconsistent
  with its 2017-2023 Multi-Year Tariff framework; the dispute has escalated
  to the Special Investment Facilitation Council (SIFC). [REPORTED —
  Business Recorder, via WebSearch synthesis only, not directly fetched —
  `[VERIFY]`]

### The mechanism: how a power-sector shortfall becomes a gas-sector shortfall

- SNGPL's own circular-debt/receivables burden reached **Rs819 billion** as
  of June 2026. Reporting attributes this primarily to historical gas price
  freezes and to the diversion of costlier imported RLNG to the domestic
  consumer sector at lower, government-set tariffs — a pricing/subsidy
  mechanism, not power-sector non-payment specifically. [REPORTED — three
  independent outlets (Profit by Pakistan Today, ProPakistani,
  EnergyUpdate.com.pk) converge on this figure and explanation; corroborated
  via a fourth outlet, Express Tribune, directly fetched. The Rs819bn figure
  itself is well-corroborated; the explanation is `[VERIFY]` pending a
  direct primary read]
- SNGPL told the government it cannot repay a Rs50 billion bank loan taken
  out for LNG payments; the government extended its sovereign loan guarantee
  to June 2027. [REPORTED — ProPakistani / EnergyUpdate.com.pk, via
  WebSearch synthesis — `[VERIFY]`]
- Gas-sector circular debt has been accumulating since FY2013, driven by
  inadequate or delayed consumer price revisions that prevented full cost
  recovery; reporting notes some improvement since November 2023 with more
  consistent price revisions. [REPORTED — Express Tribune, directly fetched]
- Separately and specifically: during April-June 2026, indigenous gas
  diverted to RLNG-based power plants was charged at Rs2,000/MMBtu instead
  of the notified RLNG price of Rs3,498-4,375/MMBtu. The Petroleum Division
  explicitly warned this "would create a revenue shortfall for SNGPL and
  hinder efforts to reduce the gas-sector circular debt" — a direct,
  quotable, government-stated link between an underpriced input to power
  generation and gas-sector circular debt. [REPORTED — EnergyUpdate.com.pk,
  directly fetched, direct quote]
- The Petroleum Division separately asked the Power Division to submit a
  "workable payment plan" for SNGPL's mounting receivables from the power
  sector, due June 30, 2026, "subject to reconciliation of the outstanding
  amounts." [REPORTED — EnergyUpdate.com.pk, directly fetched]

### SNGPL's power-sector receivables over 2025: a real, but fluctuating, number

- Feb 2025: Rs148.5 billion. Aug 2025: Rs76.5 billion. Dec 2025:
  Rs114.287 billion. These figures do not move in a simple straight line —
  they appear to rise and partially reset, consistent with periodic partial
  settlements followed by re-accumulation rather than one-directional
  growth. [REPORTED — Business Recorder / Profit by Pakistan Today /
  EnergyUpdate.com.pk / HUM News, all via WebSearch synthesis only —
  `[VERIFY]` each figure and its exact date before using in the script]
- Reporting states the root cause plainly: "inadequate fund releases by the
  Central Power Purchasing Agency (CPPA-G) to the power sector, which in
  turn delays payments to the gas utility." [REPORTED — Business Recorder,
  via WebSearch synthesis only — `[VERIFY]`, this is the single most
  load-bearing sentence for the episode's thesis and needs a direct-read
  confirmation before recording]
- Detailed breakdown of the August 2025 Rs76.5bn figure: Rs50.431bn owed by
  WAPDA's own power plants (Guddu Power Rs29.958bn, Nandipur Rs16.615bn,
  plus Muzaffargarh, Faisalabad, and Shahdra); Rs18.119bn in disputed gas
  charges; Rs31.462bn in late-payment surcharges. Independent power
  producers (IPPs) separately owed: Liberty Power Rs16.397bn, Orient Power
  Rs1.441bn, Engro Energy Rs424 million. [REPORTED — Business Recorder /
  EnergyUpdate.com.pk / HUM News, via WebSearch synthesis only — `[VERIFY]`]

### The Liberty Daharki case: a single, documented instance of the loop

- Liberty Daharki Power Limited (LDPL), a gas-fired independent power
  producer, owed SNGPL **Rs20.029 billion** as of December 9, 2025 (Rs17.812
  billion in gas arrears, Rs2 billion in unpaid upfront payments, plus
  additional unpaid instalments from June-October 2025) — far exceeding its
  Rs3.085 billion cash security deposit. SNGPL issued a final warning
  threatening to suspend LDPL's gas supply. [REPORTED — Profit by Pakistan
  Today / Business Recorder, via WebSearch synthesis only — `[VERIFY]`]
- LDPL's stated reason for non-payment: "limited payments received from the
  Central Power Purchasing Agency (CPPA-G)" — i.e., LDPL says it could not
  pay its gas supplier because the government's own power-purchasing agency
  had not paid LDPL for the electricity it generated. This is the single
  clearest documented instance of the exact mechanism the angle claims.
  [REPORTED — Business Recorder, via WebSearch synthesis only — `[VERIFY]`,
  highest priority for direct-read confirmation before scripting]
- This is not a new pattern for LDPL: a March 2023 report describes LDPL
  serving a force majeure notice under its Power Purchase Agreement after
  SNGPL previously suspended its gas supply over a payment dispute; a prior
  October 2021 PPA Amendment Agreement had established that non-supply of
  gas by SNGPL counts as an "Other Force Majeure Event" extending the PPA
  term. [UNRESOLVED — the chronology of these two events, as summarized by
  search, does not read as internally consistent (the "resolution" appears
  to predate the notice it supposedly resolves); `[VERIFY]` the actual
  sequence via a direct Business Recorder read before using this as a
  "recurring pattern" beat in the script]
- No report found in this pass confirms whether the December 2025 dispute
  was resolved, escalated to an actual supply cutoff, or remains open. The
  most recent related item found (Petroleum Division seeking a Power
  Division payment plan) is dated August 17, 2026, eight months later — a
  fresh check closer to the recording date is needed. `[VERIFY]`

### Reform history: the Gas Circular Debt Management Plan (GCDMP)

- The Petroleum Division, with the Task Force on Power Reforms and KPMG,
  developed a Gas Circular Debt Management Plan. It was presented to the
  IMF in March 2026 and again in May 2026; the IMF's third EFF review states
  the plan is expected to roll out in FY27 after approvals. [REPORTED —
  Profit by Pakistan Today / ProPakistani / EnergyUpdate.com.pk, via
  WebSearch synthesis only — `[VERIFY]`]
- The plan proposes five cash-inflow options: savings from LNG cargo
  diversion, a Rs5/liter petroleum levy, incremental E&P state-owned-company
  dividends, recovery of RLNG receivables from the power sector, and a 100%
  waiver of late-payment-surcharge/interest owed to gas producers. It aims
  to cut the fuel import bill by over $1 billion and generate Rs160 billion
  a year through LNG cargo diversion. [REPORTED — via WebSearch synthesis
  only — `[VERIFY]`]
- On the power side: the Economic Coordination Committee (ECC) approved a
  Rs200 billion supplementary grant (as government investment in DISCOs'
  equity) specifically to keep the circular debt flow within IMF-agreed
  limits, after the government itself projected a Rs491 billion increase
  over six months without intervention. The IMF had asked that the flow be
  restricted to Rs300-325 billion — a target the FY2025-26 gross flow of
  Rs364 billion still exceeded, even after the Rs200bn-plus subsidy
  intervention. [REPORTED — Express Tribune, via WebSearch synthesis only
  for the ECC/ IMF-limit figures — `[VERIFY]`; the Rs364bn gross-flow figure
  itself is directly fetched and higher-confidence]

## Counterarguments and how they are addressed

**The angle's own flagged counterargument:** gas-sector circular debt may be
primarily a pricing problem (underpriced domestic gas, import-parity
pricing) rather than a power-sector spillover.

**What the research found:** this counterargument has real force and should
not be argued away. Independent reporting attributes the larger share of
total gas-sector circular debt (Rs3.611tn) to historical price freezes and
RLNG-to-domestic-tariff diversion — a consumer-pricing mechanism distinct
from power-sector arrears. SNGPL's power-sector receivables (Rs76.5bn-
Rs148.5bn across 2025) are a real and documented — but, on a rough
same-period comparison, comparatively minor — slice of SNGPL's total
Rs819bn burden: roughly 9-14% [ESTIMATE — Claude's calculation, dividing the
2025 power-sector receivables range by the June 2026 Rs819bn total; the
figures are not from the same exact date, so this is an approximation,
`[VERIFY]` with same-date figures before using in the script]. The honest
framing (see "Thesis refinement" above) is that power-sector non-payment is
a real, worsening, and mechanically documented contributor that compounds
an already-larger pricing-driven gas circular debt — not that it is the
primary cause of the gas sector's total debt stock. Part 4 should state
this distinction explicitly rather than let the "doom loop" framing imply
the two debts are equal or interchangeable in scale.

## Stakeholder map

- **Owners / operators:** gas-fired IPPs (Liberty Daharki/Liberty Power,
  Orient Power, Engro Energy), WAPDA-owned plants (Guddu, Nandipur,
  Muzaffargarh, Faisalabad, Shahdra), K-Electric
- **Regulators:** NEPRA (power), OGRA (gas)
- **Intermediaries / capital providers:** CPPA-G (central power purchasing
  agency), SNGPL and SSGC (gas utilities), PSO (LNG/fuel importer),
  commercial banks (SNGPL's Rs50bn loan), the IMF
- **Government bodies:** Power Division, Petroleum Division, Ministry of
  Finance, the Economic Coordination Committee (ECC), the Special Investment
  Facilitation Council (SIFC)
- **Customers:** industrial and commercial gas/power consumers, domestic
  household consumers (beneficiaries of underpriced/diverted RLNG),
  DISCOs' retail electricity customers
- **Losers / externalities:** taxpayers (via repeated subsidy injections),
  future consumers (via compounding late-payment surcharges), Pakistan's FX
  reserves (LNG is imported in dollars)
