## Approved angle

**The Gwadar Promise vs. The Karachi Reality** — Gwadar's promise collided with
ground reality: shippers stayed locked into Karachi's existing logistics
ecosystem, and the connectivity and capacity Gwadar needed to actually compete
were never built at the scale required, leaving nearly all of Pakistan's seaborne
trade routed through two ports on one stretch of Karachi coastline.

## Central question

Why has Gwadar failed to divert meaningful trade volume from Karachi Port and
Port Qasim, despite years of investment as Pakistan's designated future deep-sea
port?

## Tentative thesis

Gwadar's promise collided with ground reality: shippers stayed locked into
Karachi's existing logistics ecosystem, and the connectivity and capacity Gwadar
needed to actually compete were never built at the scale required.

---

## Claims required (from the angle's sub-questions)

1. How concentrated is Pakistan's seaborne trade at Karachi Port and Port Qasim, in absolute and relative terms?
2. How little cargo does Gwadar actually move, compared to what was promised and to its own installed capacity?
3. What was Gwadar promised to become, and with what investment?
4. What connectivity and infrastructure gaps explain why cargo didn't follow the investment?
5. Is the "it just needs more time" counterargument fair, given recent (2026) growth?

### Absolute cargo volumes — Karachi Port, Port Qasim, Gwadar

- **Karachi Port Trust**: FY2024-25 cargo throughput of 53.95 million tons (a
  138-year record at the time), up 4.45% year-on-year; comprising 41.68 million
  tons dry cargo and 12.28 million tons liquid bulk, plus 2.65 million TEUs of
  containers. FY2025-26 rose further to 55.44 million tons (up 2.76%).
  [REPORTED — Profit by Pakistan Today, Business Recorder, Arab News, Dawn, Bloom
  Pakistan all report consistent figures; via WebSearch synthesis, not directly
  opened. KPT's own statistics page returned HTTP 503 on two attempts this
  session — retry before recording.]
- **Port Qasim**: FY2024-25 grand total cargo throughput of **45.305 million
  tonnes** — 13.48 million tonnes dry cargo, 19.091 million tonnes liquid cargo,
  and 12.734 million tonnes container cargo by weight. [**VERIFIED** — directly
  fetched from Port Qasim Authority's own port-performance page,
  `pqa.gov.pk/en/port-operations/port-performance`, 2026-08-22]
- **Combined Karachi Port + Port Qasim, FY2024-25**: approximately **99.3
  million tonnes** (53.95M + 45.3M). [ANALYSIS — Claude's own addition of the
  two figures above; not itself a single reported combined figure]
- **Gwadar Port**: cargo throughput of just **34,000 tonnes (0.034 million)**
  in calendar year 2024, down sharply from **592,000 tonnes (0.592 million)**
  in 2023. [REPORTED — consistent across multiple sources including Arab
  News/government statements and industry press; via WebSearch synthesis]
- Gwadar has handled a cumulative total of just **4,789 containers since
  operations began**, against an installed annual handling capacity of
  **240,000 TEUs** — a figure the government itself stated to Parliament.
  [REPORTED — cited in press coverage of a National Assembly session; via

- Gwadar's second-phase development was handed to China Overseas Port Holding
  Company (COPHC) in February 2013. [REPORTED]
- COPHC invested approximately **$1.62 billion** in Gwadar port development.
  A second figure describing a further $1.62 billion for "Phase II expansion"
  appeared in search results but looks like it may be a duplication of the same
  figure rather than two genuinely separate investment tranches.
  [**UNRESOLVED — VERIFY** the actual Phase I vs. Phase II investment figures
  before using either number in the script; do not state "$3.24 billion total"
  without confirming these are distinct amounts.]
- Broader CPEC investment pledges grew from an initial ~$46 billion to ~$62
  billion — described as roughly one-fifth of Pakistan's GDP at the time the
  figure was cited. [REPORTED — Middle East Institute analysis, via WebSearch
  synthesis]
- Gwadar's deep-water port became "formally operational" in 2016, a decade
  after first-phase construction completed, with three berths and an initial
  installed capacity of 137,000 containers/year. [REPORTED] In 2017, the port
  received only 145 TEUs of project cargo and no general commercial cargo
  vessel that year. [REPORTED]
- Gwadar Free Zone: approximately 47 enterprises registered in "Phase 1" (6
  factories built, 3 fully operational, 6 more under construction); a separate
  figure describes 35 companies registered in "Free Zone South" with 9 fully
  operational. [REPORTED — these may describe different sub-zones or different
  points in time rather than being contradictory, but this should be clarified
  — **VERIFY** before citing a single free-zone occupancy figure.] Minimum
  investment to register is $500,000 plus a $3,000 registration fee. [REPORTED]

### Why the cargo didn't follow

- The M-8 motorway (Gwadar–Ratodero, 892 km planned) remains only partially
  built. The Gwadar–Hoshab section (193 km) opened in February 2016. The
  Hoshab–Awaran section was under active construction as of 2025 with over
  Rs 25 billion allocated. Large stretches remain incomplete "despite initial
  planning dating back decades," per one source's characterisation. [REPORTED
  — SkyscraperCity/Wikipedia-sourced infrastructure tracking plus press; via
  WebSearch synthesis]
- A direct rail link connecting Gwadar to the national network (Gwadar–Mastung
  and Besima–Jacobabad) is described as a "mid-term" CPEC project scheduled to
  begin in 2025 and conclude by 2030 — meaning, as of this research date
  (August 2026), the port still has no completed direct rail connection to the
  rest of Pakistan's freight network. [REPORTED]
- In December 2024, Pakistan's Parliamentary Secretary for Planning,
  Development and Special Initiatives told the National Assembly that Gwadar
  was "fully operational" and equipped to handle all kinds of cargo — a claim
  that sits in direct, documented tension with the 34,000-tonne 2024 throughput
  and 4,789-container cumulative total reported elsewhere. [REPORTED — Arab
  News. This is a factual contradiction between two documented statements, not
  an allegation, and can be presented as such: what was said in Parliament
  next to what the government's own throughput data shows.]
- A report submitted to Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Gwadar's
  operationalization identified specific barriers: security concerns (global
  insurance firms' reluctance to cover shipments, raising insurance costs and
  discouraging international shipping lines), high operational costs, lack of
  infrastructure, and limited international commercial interest. [REPORTED —
  Profit by Pakistan Today, February 2025; via WebSearch synthesis, not
  directly opened — the underlying report itself would be a stronger primary
  source if it can be located before recording.]

### The 2026 counterargument test

- In 2026, Gwadar's cargo activity increased sharply: approximately 11,000
  containers were processed in April 2026 alone — more than the roughly 8,300
  TEUs handled in all of 2025 — and approximately 200,000 tonnes of transit
  breakbulk cargo were handled over a three-month period. Press coverage
  explicitly links this increase to "Hormuz disruption" — a regional Gulf
  shipping disruption diverting cargo away from its normal routes. [REPORTED —
  via WebSearch synthesis, including breakbulk.news industry coverage]
- **This matters directly for testing the thesis honestly**: the 2026 uptick is
  real, but it appears to be substantially driven by an external, likely
  temporary regional disruption rather than by Pakistan finally completing the
  connectivity and industrial development the original promise depended on.
  The script should present this complication directly rather than either (a)
  ignoring the 2026 growth to preserve a cleaner "failure" narrative, or (b)
  treating temporary transit-diversion cargo as proof the original promise is
  now being fulfilled.

## Counterarguments and how they are addressed

1. **"Gwadar is a young port — new ports take decades to ramp up elsewhere too."**
   Legitimate. The script should not treat low volume alone as proof of failure.
   The sharper, more defensible finding is the specific connectivity gap (M-8
   still incomplete after nearly two decades of planning, direct rail link not
   due until 2030) and the government's own acknowledged barriers (security,
   insurance cost, infrastructure), not simply "not enough cargo yet."
2. **"2026's cargo growth shows the promise is finally being realised."**
   Addressed directly in the brief above — the growth appears substantially
   tied to a regional shipping disruption (Hormuz), not to the completion of
   the connectivity Gwadar was promised. This should be stated as a genuine
   complication, not dismissed or overstated.
3. **"Karachi's dominance may reflect legitimate economic geography (established
   industrial base, population, existing logistics networks) rather than a
   policy failure at Gwadar specifically."** Fair point — the episode's
   thesis is about the connectivity and infrastructure gap specifically, not a
   claim that Gwadar could or should have equalled Karachi's volume by now.

## Stakeholder map

- **Owners/operators:** China Overseas Port Holding Company (Gwadar, 40-year
  concession), Karachi Port Trust, Port Qasim Authority
- **Regulators/funders:** Ministry of Maritime Affairs, National Highway
  Authority (M-8/N-25), Pakistan Railways (Gwadar rail link), Government of
  Balochistan, Chinese state financing (China Development Bank)
- **Capital providers:** COPHC/Chinese state investment, Government of Pakistan
  PSDP allocations, CPEC-linked financing
- **Customers:** Pakistani importers/exporters currently routed through
  Karachi Port and Port Qasim; the small number of Gwadar Free Zone enterprises
  and transit-cargo shippers
- **Losers / cost-bearers:** Balochistan's local economy, promised industrial
  and employment benefits from Gwadar that have not materialised at scale;
  Pakistani taxpayers funding continued connectivity investment with returns
  not yet realised
